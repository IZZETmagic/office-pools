SportPool · Scouting · Visual spec 02

One feature, three doors

Scouting is built and it works. What it is missing is a grammar — the three surfaces were written separately, so the same fact wears a different colour depending on which door you came through. This is the shared vocabulary that turns three screens into one feature, and the scope rule that fixes the cold start.

A colour must mean exactly one thing across the whole feature. Before this, gold meant five things, green four, and the same home/draw/away split was drawn three different ways inside a single sheet.

The same split, three encodings, one sheet

A home / draw / away share appears twice in the binoculars sheet and once on the match detail tab. The pairing bar draws it green · silver · red. Two cards below, the crowd bar draws it blue · silver · gold. The match detail tab draws it in club colours.

So in the pairing bar green means Arsenal and red means Chelsea — a value judgement the card is explicitly not making — and one scroll later gold has stopped being “the finding” and become Chelsea.

Red is carrying a privacy note

The crowd card ends with “{n} picks from across every pool on SportPool — never your own” on a red tint. That line is the feature’s best reassurance, and red is the colour this same sheet uses for a defeat four cards earlier.

It reads as an error state on the one card that most needs to feel trustworthy. This is the single cheapest fix in the document.

The rating pill is decorative

Every player rating renders green, so green there carries no information at all — a 6.2 and an 8.4 get the same treatment. Green is doing work in the form strip one card above, which makes this actively costly rather than merely neutral.

02

The grammar — one job per colour

Five colours, five jobs, no overlap. The rule that makes it hold: a colour may only be spent on the job named below, and if a card needs a sixth meaning it does not get a colour — it gets a label.

GreenTHE FOOTBALL WENT WELL

A win. Only ever a result that actually happened, from the point of view of the side being described.

Used in: form strips, past-meeting chips. Nowhere else.

RedTHE FOOTBALL WENT BADLY

A defeat, or a genuinely negative fact about a member — a missed pick, a blind spot.

Never: a club, a scope note, a privacy disclaimer, or anything the reader has not lost by.

GoldTHE FINDING

The one line on this card worth reading aloud. Exactly one per card, and a card with no finding has no gold on it.

Used in: the drought, the signature scoreline, the reality mark, the top-rated player.

AmberREAD IT LIGHTLY

A caveat about the sample, and nothing else. Amber is the feature admitting its own limits, which is why it must never also be a statistic.

Moves off amber: the contrarian index, the drought’s visit count.

BlueTHE SUBJECT

Whoever this report is about — the home club in a fixture split, the member in a dossier comparison. The thing being measured, as opposed to what it is measured against.

Its opposite is gold: blue is them, gold is reality.

Blue / silver / inkTHE THREE-WAY SPLIT

Home, draw, away — one encoding, every split, every surface. Position and the key beneath it carry the identity; the colours are only a lookup.

Replaces: green/silver/red, blue/silver/gold and club colours, all three.

The payoff is a comparison that does not exist today

Once the history bar and the crowd bar share an encoding, they can sit directly on top of one another — and the reader sees, in one glance, that history says home and the crowd says away. That is the most interesting thing the scout report knows, and at the moment it is unreadable purely because the two bars are drawn in different colours four cards apart. Cohesion here is not tidiness; it buys a finding.

This is a deletion, not a build

ScoutingTab.tsx goes. Extract the sheet’s body as <ScoutReport/> and both doors render it. The match screen drops from two calls (/h2h + /players) to one (/scout) and gains the form and crowd cards it does not currently have.

The World Cup blocker turned out not to exist

This section used to warn that /scout 404s on a World Cup match. It does — but so do /h2h and /players, and useMatchDetail only calls them inside if (leagueMatch). The Scouting tab was already league-only in practice, so the swap needed no fallback at all.

And the tab already had a crowd card

Hidden inside match/PeopleCard.tsx — a second copy of the same bar, with the same blue/silver/gold encoding. So the count of duplicated crowd bars was two, not one. The tab's real gaps were the venue-split form card, the drought and the thin-sample caveat; it gained all three.

One header, two subjects

The dossier header now takes the fixture header’s shape exactly: eyebrow, subject row, detail line, hairline rule, left-aligned. Only the subject slot differs — two crests and a “v” for a match, one avatar for a member.

Which means the member’s colour survives the change. The fixture header carries identity through its crests; the person equivalent is the avatar inline beside the name, keyed on their user id and frozen, so they are still the same colour here, in Banter and on the duel card. It is the same mechanism, not a lost one.

The rest follows for free: card edges, title rows, row rhythm, the gold finding, the amber caveat and the slate footnote are all the same objects. Somebody who has learned that gold means “this is the line” on a fixture already knows it on a person.

The scope tag is new, and it is the fix for the cold start

Every card now states what it is counted over. Three cards say This pool and two say All time, and that is not a toggle the member sets — it is the correct scope for that particular statistic. Section 06 is the argument.

What the header change costs, and one trap in its detail line

The purple wash goes. DossierSheet’s 220pt gradient was the single biggest visual difference between the two sheets, and it is what “identical” costs. If it is missed, the cheapest way back is a hairline rule in the member’s colour under the header — but the fixture header has two clubs and no single colour, so it could not follow.

The detail line now has a standing slot, and Last Man Standing must not fill it. The stored current_rank in LMS is entry-id order, not a position — the venue line is safe to be a rank on a fixture and is not safe to be one on a member. It needs a mode guard before it ships, the same one the pool card KPIs already carry.

Two smaller calls baked into the mockup

The accuracy tiles drop tierWinner cyan and tierExact gold — those are the scoring tier colours and neither tile is showing a tier. The headline tile takes gold instead, and the rest are ink.

The player rating pill was green for every rating — 6.2 and 8.4 alike — so green carried no information on the one card where it was also load-bearing two cards above. It is neutral now, and the leader of the list takes a heavier wash of the same neutral rather than a colour. Hierarchy without spending gold. Green goes back to meaning a win.

06

The lifetime question

The instinct is right — the dossier is thin in a new pool. But “make it lifetime” has a trap in it, and the fix is better than a toggle.

First — it is not the pick count that is thin

By matchweek 3 an entry already has ~30 picks. That is plenty for a hit rate, a goals-per-prediction, a draw rate — every card in Against reality works from week 3.

What is thin is the per-club count. Each club appears once a matchweek, so at MW3 you have seen Liverpool exactly three times. MIN_BACKINGS is 2 and MIN_BLIND_SPOT_BACKINGS is 3, so Club bias technically renders — but “backs Liverpool 3 of 3” is noise. The design note’s own example, 9 of 9, needs matchweek nine.

So the dossier does not go from empty to full. It fills in layers, and only one card is actually waiting on time.

Second — a naive union double-counts, badly

A member in two Premier League pools picks the same fixture twice. Union their rows and Liverpool-at-home goes from 8 of 8 to 16 of 16 — the same judgement counted twice, presented as twice the evidence.

Worse when the two picks disagree: back Liverpool in one pool and against them in the other, and the lifetime figure reports a considered ambivalence that never existed.

59.5%

of the league picks in production today are a repeat of a (user, fixture) pair already counted.

766 prediction rows resolve to 310 independent judgements across 11 users. A lifetime dossier built by unioning rows would inflate every denominator by roughly 2.5×.

⚠ These are the seeded UX-test pools, so the figure is not a forecast of real behaviour — those users sit in more pools than a member will. The mechanism is real for anyone in two pools in one competition, which is exactly the member lifetime is meant to serve. Dedupe on (user_id, fixture_id), and decide explicitly what happens when two picks disagree — dropping the fixture is the honest answer.

The rule: how someone picks is a lifetime trait. How they are doing is a pool fact.

That single sentence assigns every statistic a scope without a setting, and it is defensible to a member in one line. A duel is decided inside a pool, so the competitive numbers stay there; character does not reset when you join a new pool, so the tendencies do not either.

Card Scope Why
Standing & duel record This pool A rank only exists inside a pool. A lifetime rank is meaningless and, in Last Man Standing, the stored one is entry-id order anyway.
Accuracy This pool Pool depth changes what a point is worth. A Results pool and a Scores pool do not share a points scale, so a lifetime average is arithmetic over two different units.
Recent matchweeks This pool It is a timeline of this season in this competition. Interleaving two pools’ matchweeks produces an order that never happened.
Club bias All time The card lifetime exists for. Needs ~9 sightings of one club; one pool takes half a season to get there, three seasons of history gets there on day one.
Against reality All time Draw-blindness and goal optimism are traits, not form. They are also the most stable numbers in the dossier, so a wider sample makes them better rather than staler.
Contrarian index All time Measured against the platform crowd, which is already platform-wide — scoping the member half to one pool compares two different populations.
Missed picks This pool A competitive fact about this duel. Also the denominator must start at their join date per pool, or a member who joined in MW6 is reported as having missed five weeks.

The seal still has to be re-implemented

readOpponentPicks filters on league_matchweeks.lock_at, which generalises correctly across competitions — different leagues lock at different times and the join handles it. Do not drop that filter when widening the query from an entry to a user. It is the whole seal, and this route runs on the admin client.

One genuinely new exposure: a bigger denominator implicitly reveals that the subject plays in other pools. That is mild, but it means the card may never name another pool or break a figure down by one.

“All time” means all league pools, at first

World Cup picks live in predictions, not league_predictions — a different table with a different shape. A lifetime dossier that spans both is a union across two schemas, and it is not worth doing for a competition that closed in July. Say all leagues on the card and mean it.

And for a genuinely new member, scope does not help at all

Somebody in their first week has no picks anywhere, and no amount of widening invents any. That state needs to be a real screen, not an empty one: the standing and the duel record still exist, and the honest line is “No book on them yet — 4 picks so far.”

Which is itself interesting in a duel. Facing an unknown is a fact worth stating plainly.

08

What the build turned up

Three things this document got wrong, and four findings it did not know about. The first has been firing in production the whole time; the second has not fired yet.

90%

of goals were landing on nobody, and the Danger list was ranking on assists.

match_events files a scorer as K. Havertz. match_player_stats files the same man as Kai Havertz. One provider, two endpoints, two naming conventions — and scoutSide joined them on exact string equality.

Measured against production: 417 of 463 goals matched nobody. They did not error and they did not duplicate. They landed on no one, the player rendered with zero goals, and a squad’s actual top scorer routinely did not appear on the card about danger.

The file carried a comment asserting the opposite — that a two-way spelling “would show a player twice, which is a visible bug rather than a silent one”. Both halves were wrong. playerNameKey() now folds both to <initial>. <rest>, unaccented; 426 of 443 join. ⚠ Known limit, written into the code: two team-mates sharing an initial and a surname pool their goals, and match_events carries no player id to disambiguate with.

Which is why “are the two lists the same?” could not be answered until it was fixed

The overlap between In form and Danger, measured across 96 clubs, moved once goals actually attached: shared names went from 0.77 of 3 to 1.11, clubs sharing nobody fell from 44% to 23%, and clubs sharing two or more rose from 19% to 34%.

So the split was never redundant — two thirds of names still appeared in only one list. The decision to fold them into one ranking of five is therefore a choice about how much a reader should hold at once mid-pick, not a removal of duplication. Each row carries goals and assists, so a scorer stays visible; he is ranked by how he has played rather than by what he has scored.

⚠ The cost is stated rather than hidden: the 180-minute floor now governs the whole card, so a striker with four goals in three starts does not appear at all. The danger list was his way on.

2027

Club bias would have split Arsenal in two the day the next season is imported.

buildClubLeans grouped on club.clubId, and league_clubs is UNIQUE (season_id, external_club_id) — Arsenal gets a fresh club_id every season, because importLeagueSeason inserts a new row per season and never reuses one.

So a lifetime “backs Arsenal 9 of 9” would silently become “5 of 5” and “4 of 4”, and neither half would win the mostBacked contest against a club the member only played one season of. Not a missing club — a confidently wrong winner.

Latent today: production holds five competitions but only one season year, so no two seasons share a club — which also settles Open Question 1 of the 09-09 design note. Live the day 2027/28 lands, which is the next thing on the roadmap. Fixed by carrying externalClubId on ClubRef, with a test that fails against the old grouping.

A late joiner was being reported for weeks they could not pick

countAvailableFixtures counted every locked fixture in the season, so somebody who joined at matchweek six was told they had missed all of one to five.

Missed picks is the one dossier stat justified on the grounds that it is “a competitive fact — a missed pick has already changed results”, and it is the number an opponent is most likely to repeat out loud. The denominator now starts at the entry’s join date.

⚠ The conflict rule may be too strict — watch this one

Where two pools hold different picks for one fixture, the fixture is dropped. On the seeded test pools 180 of the 310 deduped fixtures come back as disagreements — so lifetime would yield fewer usable picks than the pool alone, and the route correctly falls back to pool scope rather than shipping a worse dossier.

Those seeded picks are random, so the ratio says nothing about real members. But if real members do diverge across pools, the better rule is to count the fixture in seen and not in backed — preserving the opportunity while refusing to claim a direction. Worth revisiting on real data.

The guard test earned its place immediately

It caught StandingCard — a scouting file reaching for green, red and gold directly, which the port had missed entirely. That is exactly the failure the guard exists for: no single file was wrong, and it was only visible across files.

It also refused its own first draft, because kit/tone.ts did not match the pattern it was banning. The positive half of a guard is what stops the ban passing for the wrong reason.

Two decisions taken during the build

No rank in the dossier header

The §05 mockup drew “3rd of 12” into the detail line. Ryan had removed the rank from that header on 10 Sep — “a pool position is not what this one is about” — so the earlier decision was kept. It is also the safe call: in Last Man Standing the stored current_rank is entry-id order, so a rank there would be wrong for a whole mode. StandingCard still shows it, behind its own guard.

“No book yet” is derived, not a threshold

The obvious version is picks < 10. That would be a fourth sample floor beside the three the server already owns, and the two would drift. Instead the screen asks whether every card came back empty — so it can never disagree with what the server was willing to say.
