SportPool · Design concept · 2026-09-09

Scouting

A scout report attaches to a decision, not to a page. Wherever the product asks a member to commit — a fixture pick, a duel, an LMS survival pick, a table prediction — scouting is the panel that says here is what has actually happened before. There is no Scouting tab in the nav.

The rule that governs every stat

Scouting reports what happened. It never forecasts what will happen.

“Most common scoreline in this fixture” is a memory. “Most likely score” is a tip. The second is a betting product with the odds filed off — and this product is explicitly not for bettors. One word decides which one we ship.

Why the venue split is layer 2, not a footnote

Layer 1 is the one that goes missing. Layer 2 is the one that is always there — every club plays home and away every season — so it is the load-bearing fallback for the entire report. Design it first; it is what renders when nothing else can.

Layer 3 costs nothing today

Migration 141 landed four days ago and put rating, minutes, goals, assists, key passes and duels into a real indexable table — at zero additional provider cost, because the bytes already arrive in the /fixtures?ids= bundle we pay for. Nothing reads it yet. Current-season player scouting is a GROUP BY.

Layer 4 has a hard boundary

Crowd data must be platform-wide. A crowd stat scoped to your own pool leaks your pool-mates’ picks through the back door of an aggregate, and defeats both the weekly reveal and the sealed Showdown draw. This is a constraint, not a preference — it should be enforced in SQL, not in the component.

The streak sentence is the product

“Chelsea have not won at the Emirates since 2011” is one derived line and it is worth more than the twelve numbers above it. It is the thing that gets screenshotted into Banter, which is where ~67% of banter traffic already comes from.

Hard rule: no percentage below n = 5

“50% of the time” on a sample of two is a lie with a decimal point on it. The ladder is explicit: 0 → skip layer 1 · 1–2 → anecdotes only, scorelines and dates, never a rate · 3–4 → anecdotes plus counts · 5+ → rates unlock.

Why an empty state would be the wrong answer

A card that says “no data” reads as broken. A sentence — “these two have never met” — reads as news, and it is genuinely useful information about a fixture. The existing HeadToHead component in DuelsTab.tsx already makes exactly this argument for first meetings in the round-robin; scouting inherits the voice.

It is also what makes it chargeable

A stats product that oversells thin samples is one a member loses a pick to and never trusts again — which in a product whose stated purpose is no bad feelings is the whole ballgame. The n-rule is a trust feature before it is an honesty one.

Phase 0 — the whole screen, zero provider cost

Nothing here needs an API call, a backfill, or a season of history. It runs on picks the product already stores. It is the most differentiated part of the system and the cheapest, which is why the build order puts it first.

The blind spot is the best line in the set

“Picks Man Utd to win 8 times. Right twice.” It is useful, it is funny, and critically it is a fact about football rather than about a person — which is what keeps it on the right side of banter rather than the wrong side of it.

Draw rate vs reality is the sleeper

Roughly a quarter of league games end level and almost nobody predicts them at anything near that rate. Putting a member’s 6% next to the real 25% is the single most revealing thing in the dossier, and it applies to every player in the product.

Why pick timing is excluded

Run it through the disclosure gate: “We show your opponent what time of day you usually make your picks, and how often you change your mind.” That is surveillance, not scouting. It has never changed a result, and it would push people to pick early purely to hide the signal.

The line: a scouting stat describes a revealed decision that affected a result — not the behaviour around making it. Missed-pick rate passes. Pick timing does not.

It extends what exists

DuelsTab.tsx already ships a Tale of the Tape and a HeadToHead meeting list. The dossier is that card grown up — and it inherits its opponentVisible gate, so the sealed half of a cycle never shows a dossier for somebody you have finished playing.

Self-scouting is the strongest retention mechanic here — without being one

It is just your own data, shown plainly. It passes the disclosure gate trivially because there is no mechanism to disclose. And “you have never predicted a 0–0 and there have been eleven” is the kind of line that makes somebody change how they play.

LMS is where scouting is worth the most

In pick’em a bad pick costs you points. In Last Man Standing it ends your season. The opportunity-cost framing — “spending City here costs you their easiest remaining fixture” — is the single most valuable thing the scout can say anywhere in the product.

Two LMS-specific traps

Never show a rank on an LMS surface — the stored current_rank is entry-id order, not standing. And club position comes from ingested standings, never recomputed from points, because a derived table cannot see a points deduction.

Share cards are already ~67% of Banter traffic. A scout report is inherently shareable and should be built to be — the derived sentence is the payload, the numbers are the receipt.

Six surfaces, one computed object

The report is the same shape everywhere it appears. That is the whole argument for computing it in Postgres: a scout report has forty numbers in it and it cannot be derived twice.

The duel-scale precedent

One constant — the 500/250/0 duel scale — had three independent readers, and nine settled duels displayed wrong for four days. Nothing errored. Scouting has forty constants and six surfaces. lib/scouting/ owns every threshold and every bucket boundary, with a guard test that scans for a second implementation.

07

The paywall has a fairness problem

One issue has to be settled before anything else is built.

The problem

If one player in a Showdown duel can see the dossier and the other cannot, the competition is no longer fair.

That is pay-to-win inside a head-to-head contest between two named friends — the most direct possible contradiction of no bad feelings, and the same worry the vision doc already records about paywalling banter and XP.

The rule that resolves it

You never pay to see something about another person that they cannot see about you.

LayerModelWhy
Self-scouting Always free It is your own data. Charging for it feels like a hostage.
Opponent scouting Pool-level unlock Symmetry is preserved — the admin buys it and every member gets it. It also monetises better: one buyer, whole pool, and the buyer is the person most invested. It makes the admin a hero rather than making one member an advantaged outsider.
Fixture & club scouting Per-user tiering OK Public information about football, not about another member. Free = layers 1–2 headline; paid = depth (player level, splits, crowd).
08

Build order

Ordered by value delivered per provider-dollar spent, which puts the free half first. Only one line needs a budget conversation, and it is last for that reason.

PhaseWhat Provider costBlocked on
0 Opponent + self dossier — the whole of §03 and half of §04, from picks already stored None league_entry_analytics — the league sibling of the World Cup entryAnalytics.ts, which reads a table that is empty for every league entry
1 Fixture form, venue-split — layer 2, the load-bearing fallback None A season-independent clubs entity. Arsenal currently has a different club_id every season
2 H2H pairing history — layer 1, via /fixtures/headtohead ~380 calls, once A club_pair_history cache. Immutable once played, so it is written once and never refreshed
3 Player level, current season — layer 3, from migration 141 None A players entity; the open is_starter bug
4 The crowd layer — layer 4 None The reveal boundary enforced in SQL, not in the component
5 Historical player depth — full prior-season backfill Expensive Only if phases 0–4 prove out. This is the budget conversation

Open questions

  1. How many seasons are in production? Unverified — the importer takes a year argument, but nothing in the repo shows it has been run for a past season. One query. It decides whether phase 2 is a cache or a backfill.
  2. Is the pool-level unlock acceptable? Or is per-user tiering wanted despite the fairness cost?
  3. Does the crowd layer stay platform-wide only? Or is there an appetite for an in-pool version after reveal?
  4. Is pick timing genuinely out? It is the one stat with real competitive value that the disclosure gate rejects — and that call is yours, not mine.
