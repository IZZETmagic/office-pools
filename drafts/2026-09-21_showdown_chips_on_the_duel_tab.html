What reading the built tab actually decided

❌ It cannot go on the Tale of the Tape

That was my first instinct — the Tape is the input to the decision, so hang the chips off its foot. The file refuses it in writing (DuelTab.tsx:1319):

"EVERY ROW IS A COMPARISON, so the winning side is BOLDED rather than labelled — the shape of the card is the comparison. Adding a 'leader' chip to each row would say the same thing twice and take the width to say it."

A chips footer is not a comparison. It would be the first thing on that card that isn't.

✅ It goes 4th, after "What it will be decided on"

The tab states its own pre-lock logic: "your sheet, the opponent scouted, and the fixtures the duel will be decided on — every one of those cards exists to inform a pick."

A chip doesn't inform a pick; it's a separate decision about the stake. So it sits after that trio and before the records — you've seen the tape, scouted them, and seen the ten games, and only then are you asked whether to raise.

It also keeps the "Finish your picks" button at position 2, out of the chip's way. That is the whole banker defence made physical: the route to picking never passes through a chip.

⭐ The band needs no change at all

The band shows accuracy_a/_b, never points_a/_b — "500–0 is not a scoreline anybody played". A chip changes the payout, never the accuracy, so the most expensive surface in the mode is untouched by Double Stakes and Take the Room.

That matters because the band is not safely editable: COLLAPSED_SPREAD = 108 is derived from the live score's widest half, and MIDDLE_COL = 160 only just clears HH:MM:SS. Ryan tuned those by eye over ~20 rounds. Do not put a chip mark in the collapsed row.

The card wears CardHeader, like everything else

DuelTab.tsx:1524: "Six cards each inventing their own heading is how a tab reads as six screens that happen to be stacked." So: title = "Your chips", meta = the count hard right, subtitle = the one line of disclosure.

⭐ The meta slot is exactly where the expiry belongs — "3 left · to MW19". The whole renew/expire rule fits in the header shape the tab already wears, with no new furniture invented for it.

Icons all exist in the map already: bolt.fill, person.2.fill, target (binoculars is taken, by scouting).

⚠ Only The Call-Out touches the in-play surface

Double Stakes and Take the Room are invisible once football starts. The Call-Out has to mark one row of the team sheet — and that row already has a fixed shape (TeamSheetRows, shared web/mobile via lib/league/duelSheet.ts + its byte mirror). A marked row is a real change to a mirrored, guard-tested module on both platforms.

It's also the most watchable thing here: the called-out row is the one you refresh for.

⚠ Web and mobile already disagree about card order

RN leads with the Tape (Ryan, 2026-09-03: "the tape leads"). Web puts Your sheet first and the Tape fourth (DuelsTab.tsx:1554 vs :1697).

So "4th" isn't one answer. Either place the chips card relative to "What it will be decided on" on both — which is what the mockup does — or decide the two tabs are allowed to differ, as the two walkouts already are.

The halfway rule — what it fixes, and the one place it can fail

✅ It fixes the real failure mode of a season chip

A once-a-season chip gets hoarded and never played — everyone waits for the perfect week and the perfect week never announces itself. Halving the term forces the decision twice and takes the volume to six a season, about one every six matchweeks. That's a rhythm rather than a rare event, which is also what makes it teachable: you see someone else do it before your turn comes.

It resets everyone equally regardless of standing, so it is a catch-up beat that isn't rich-get-richer. FPL landed on the same answer for the same reason.

⚠ The mechanic passes gate 2. A notification about it would not.

This is the whole risk and it is worth being exact about. The expiry itself is fine — disclosed up front, costs you nothing you earned, and the deadline is the competition's own halfway point rather than a clock we invented.

"⏰ Your Double Stakes expires in 2 days — use it or lose it!" is manufactured FOMO and fails gate 2 outright. The line: the count lives on the card, permanently, non-escalating, and it never leaves the app. State 4 above turns one word amber and adds one clause. That is the entire treatment.

⚠ It also has to survive its own tooltip, and it does: "Three chips each half of the season. Unused ones don't carry over."

🔴 "Halfway" is not defined yet — and migration 143 is why

pools.league_start_matchweek means a pool can start at any matchweek. So:

Season halfway (MW19) is sport-inherited and identical for everyone — but a pool starting at MW25 gets zero first-half chips and then a full second set. Broken.

Pool halfway (midpoint of the pool's own matchweek span) always gives two equal halves. My recommendation — and it must be resolved to a named matchweek at pool creation ("your chips renew at Matchweek 20"), never a rolling clock. That satisfies migration 128's standing rule: an interval from a football event, never an hour of the day.

⚠ Floor it. A pool starting at MW36 has three matchweeks and a halfway of 1.5. Below ~10 matchweeks, don't renew at all — one set for the whole pool.

⚠ A chip you could not have played must not expire

If your last matchweek before halfway is a bye (entry_b IS NULL), every chip here is dead — there is no opponent to raise against and a bye pays a flat 250 regardless. Expiring a chip you were never able to spend is us taking something back for a reason that is ours, not football's.

Rule: an unplayable half rolls one chip forward. Disclosed in the same sentence.

Knock-on: the Handshake gets more dangerous

Six chips a season instead of three makes the draw-offer's collusion case materially easier to farm. If the Handshake survives at all, it should stay one per season while the other three renew — or be dropped.

The Return Fixture is unaffected: it spends no chip.

What this does to the numbers

Six chips × ~7.6 members ≈ 46 chip-weeks a season in a typical pool, across 38 matchweeks and ~5 round-robin cycles. So roughly one duel in eight carries a chip — common enough to be part of the mode's rhythm, rare enough that it is still an event when the band lights up.

⚠ Worth deciding on purpose: ShowdownLadder's Duels board sorts duel_points ↓ → wins ↓ → season total ↓. Six doubled duels a season means duel_points and wins come apart far more often, so that second rung starts doing real work for the first time.
